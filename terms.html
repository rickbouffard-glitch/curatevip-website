Attorney note — automatic renewal compliance

A large share of members will be California residents, so California's Automatic Renewal Law is the key compliance risk. It regulates the checkout screen and the post-purchase confirmation email, not just this page: the renewal terms must appear in visual proximity to the purchase button, the member must separately consent to them, and a retainable acknowledgment with cancellation instructions must be sent after purchase. Please review the live checkout flow and confirmation email against §17601–17604, plus New York GBL §527-a and the FTC negative-option rules.

Attorney note — arbitration clause needs review (client decision)

The Delaware governing law and Delaware AAA arbitration choices have been kept as-is, but three gaps should be resolved before publication:

  • No class-action waiver. The clause compels arbitration but never waives class or collective proceedings and does not address class arbitration. Decide whether to add an express waiver plus a severability / blow-up provision.
  • No arbitration opt-out window. There is no 30-day right to reject arbitration. It is not strictly required, but its absence is weighed in California's procedural unconscionability analysis and an opt-out is near-standard in consumer clauses.
  • Delaware forum against California consumers. Requiring a Californian who paid $299 to arbitrate in Delaware is a common substantive unconscionability challenge, and a Delaware choice of law that strips CLRA / ARL remedies runs into California's anti-waiver statutes. Consider remote hearings, the consumer's home county, or company-paid AAA consumer fees.

Also consider a small-claims carve-out, an injunctive-relief carve-out for IP, a mass-arbitration batching protocol, and confirming the acceptance flow is a genuine clickwrap.
