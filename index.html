The Problem

Searching for luxury hotels and restaurants ends in homework.

You know what you like. Finding it is the problem: hours of sorting, reviews written by people who travel nothing like you, photographs studied one at a time, trying to work out whether a room will actually meet your standard.

We get it. We do the same thing. Every tool out there is built for everyone at once, the $200 night and the $2,000 night, rated on the same five stars, averaged from thousands of people who don't travel or dine the way you do. And the top results on most sites aren't the best places. They're the ones that paid to be there.

The first result is an ad
Search "luxury hotels" in any city and the top of the page is paid placement (one of them advertising a "Big Summer Sale"). Luxury doesn't go on clearance. You're being sold to, not advised.
65 listings that look identical
One search drops you into dozens of near-identical five-star results. Even Amex Travel, the card built for luxury, opens with 287 hotels in a single city and still leaves the sorting to you.
A "luxury" guide listing a Motel 6
One site advertising luxury hotels returns 80 results on page one, with a Motel 6 sitting beside two genuinely exceptional resorts. A single review point, 7.5 against 8.5, is the only guidance on offer, and it tells you nothing about which is yours.

"If I'm committing my time and my money to this place, is it actually going to be my speed?"

Right now, you're guessing. That's the whole problem.

The Idea

A Speed is how a place feels, not how much it costs.

Two hotels can both be excellent, both be expensive, and be completely wrong for each other's guests. The difference isn't quality. It's atmosphere. These are the Speeds we match you to.

Where you rest
The Sanctuary: relaxation is everything. Quiet, restorative, unhurried.

The Atelier: architecture is the reason you booked it. Design-led, considered.

The Estate: history and charm. A property with a past worth staying in.
Where you dine
The Provision: the food, the chef, and the wine are the point.

The Couture: the room is the reservation. Aesthetic, design, space.

The Pulse: energy is the product. The dining room hums.
Where the night goes
The Gallery: design you feel before you see it.

The Shadow: mood is the amenity. You had to know it was there.

The Electric: scene-driven. The right room changes the night.

Your Speed can differ across all three: restful hotel, loud table, quiet nightcap. Most people's do.
